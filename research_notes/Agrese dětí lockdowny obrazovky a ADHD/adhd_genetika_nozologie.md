## 1. Heritability and genetic architecture: what they do and do not imply

### Takeaway
ADHD is among the most heritable psychiatric conditions (twin h² ≈ 0.74 on meta-analysis; 0.88 for clinically diagnosed ADHD in Swedish registers). Its genetic architecture is highly polygenic: thousands of common variants of tiny effect (SNP-h² 0.14-0.22), plus a contribution from rare variants. The same genetic factors act across the whole symptom distribution, so ADHD behaves like the extreme of a continuous trait, much as hypertension is the extreme of blood pressure. Heritability is a population statistic about variance. It does not say that ADHD is fixed in an individual, and MTA data show symptom expression that fluctuates with the environment. High heritability is fully compatible with environmental causes of phenocopies (deprivation) and with diagnosis rates that change over time.

### Cited Findings
**Twin/family heritability**
- **Faraone SV, Larsson H. Genetics of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder. *Mol Psychiatry* 2019;24:562-575. DOI 10.1038/s41380-018-0070-0.** Narrative review of family, twin, adoption, linkage, GWAS and CNV studies. ADHD "runs in families", and its heritability is 74%. Linkage studies show that individual risk variants must have very small effects. GWAS show "about a third of ADHD's heritability is due to a polygenic component comprising many common variants each having small effects". Rare CNVs (insertions/deletions) account for part of the heritability. Not retracted (Scite). — [Faraone & Larsson 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-018-0070-0)
- **Larsson H, et al. Childhood ADHD as an extreme of a continuous trait: a quantitative genetic study of 8,500 twin pairs. *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2012;53(1):73-80. DOI 10.1111/j.1469-7610.2011.02467.x.** Design: Swedish population twin study (CATSS) of all 9- and 12-year-old twins born 1992-2000, with parent DSM-IV symptom interviews; response rate 80%. Results: DeFries-Fulker extremes analyses gave "almost identical group heritability estimates around .60" for the diagnostic cut-off (prevalence 1.78%) and the screening cut-off (prevalence 9.75%). Conclusion: ADHD is "the quantitative extreme of genetic and environmental factors operating dimensionally throughout the distribution". Limitation: research diagnoses based on parent report only. — [Larsson et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2011.02467.x)
- **Larsson H, Chang Z, D'Onofrio BM, Lichtenstein P. The heritability of clinically diagnosed ADHD across the lifespan. *Psychol Med* 2014;44:2223-2229. DOI 10.1017/S0033291713002493.** Swedish Twin Registry, 59,514 twins born 1959-2001, linked to the Prescribed Drug Register and National Patient Register. Heritability was 0.88 (95% CI 0.83-0.92) overall and 0.72 (0.56-0.84) in adults; shared environment was "non-significant and of minimal importance". The authors attribute earlier low adult estimates to self-rating (rater) effects. — [Larsson et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291713002493)

**GWAS / SNP heritability / polygenicity**
- **Demontis D, et al. Discovery of the first genome-wide significant risk loci for ADHD. *Nat Genet* 2019;51:63-75. DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0269-7.** GWAS meta-analysis (PGC + iPSYCH) of 20,183 cases and 35,191 controls, mostly European ancestry. Found 12 genome-wide significant loci. Liability-scale SNP heritability h²SNP = 0.216 (SE 0.014), assuming 5% population prevalence (Scite full-text excerpt). Associations are enriched in evolutionarily constrained regions and in loss-of-function-intolerant genes. "Strong concordance with GWAS of quantitative population measures of ADHD symptoms supports that clinical diagnosis of ADHD is an extreme expression of continuous heritable traits." Scite flags one contrasting citation (Østergaard 2020, see G×E below). — [Demontis 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-018-0269-7)
- **Demontis D, et al. Genome-wide analyses of ADHD identify 27 risk loci, refine the genetic architecture and implicate several cognitive domains. *Nat Genet* 2023;55:198-208. DOI 10.1038/s41588-022-01285-8.** 38,691 cases and 186,843 controls (iPSYCH, deCODE, PGC). Found 27 loci and 76 candidate risk genes, enriched in early brain development and midbrain dopaminergic neurons. Rare variants: in exome data from 17,896 individuals there was an increased load of rare protein-truncating variants in a set of risk genes, and SORCS3 was implicated by both common and rare variants. Bivariate mixture modelling estimated that "84-98% of ADHD-influencing variants are shared with other psychiatric disorders". Common-variant risk was associated with poorer verbal reasoning and executive functions. **A correction was published (notice DOI 10.1038/s41588-023-01350-w, March 2023, per Scite).** — [Demontis 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01285-8)
- SNP heritability in Demontis 2023 was 14% overall. It differed by cohort: iPSYCH 23%, PGC 12%, deCODE 8.1%. Source: a review by Demontis et al. that summarises its own study (Scite full-text excerpt). — [Demontis et al. 2023, *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*, DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105313](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105313)
- A secondary statement in a citing paper says Demontis 2023 estimated that "around 7000 variants could explain up to 90% of SNP-based heritability". This comes from a citing paper, not the primary text. — [de Matos et al. 2026, *Am J Med Genet B*, DOI 10.1002/ajmg.b.70028](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.b.70028)
- **Thapar A. Discoveries on the genetics of ADHD in the 21st century. *Am J Psychiatry* 2018;175:943-950. DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.18040383.** Selective review. ADHD "can be viewed as a trait"; GWAS, consistent with twin studies, "highlight that ADHD lies at the extreme end of a continuously distributed dimension, akin to hypertension along the continuum of blood pressure". A persistent trajectory is linked to higher genetic loading. "Common gene variants for ADHD are only weakly predictive and therefore have limited clinical value at present"; routine rare-mutation testing is not yet recommended. There is strong genetic overlap with autism and intellectual disability. — [Thapar 2018](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.18040383)

**What heritability means and does not mean**
- **Visscher PM, Hill WG, Wray NR. Heritability in the genomics era: concepts and misconceptions. *Nat Rev Genet* 2008;9:255-266. DOI 10.1038/nrg2322.** Heritability is "an estimable, dimensionless population parameter" that compares the relative importance of genes and environment "to the variation of traits within and across populations". It has a long history of "continuous misunderstandings and controversies". — [Visscher et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrg2322)
- In the MTA cohort (Sibley 2022, *Am J Psychiatry*, see Q6), the authors write that "the high heritability of ADHD is well established, and we speculate that genetic risks for ADHD may reflect a propensity for symptom expression that is dependent on environmental factors (e.g., changes in teachers, living arrangements, academic setting...)". They describe the fluctuating patterns as revealing "ADHD to be a dynamic rather than a static disorder" (Scite full-text). — [Sibley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21010032)
- Rydell et al. 2018 (Swedish twins; see Q5) found that clinically diagnosed ADHD rose more than fivefold between 2004 and 2014, while the parent-rated diagnostic-level phenotype did not change significantly. The rise in diagnoses therefore happened without any change in genes or in the measured extreme phenotype. — [Rydell et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12882)

**Gene-environment correlation (rGE) and interaction (G×E)**
- **Leppert B, et al. Association of maternal neurodevelopmental risk alleles with early-life exposures. *JAMA Psychiatry* 2019;76:834-842. DOI 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0774.** ALSPAC (UK), 7,921 genotyped mothers. Maternal ADHD PRS was associated with prenatal infections (OR 1.11, 95% CI 1.04-1.18), acetaminophen use in late pregnancy (OR 1.11, 1.04-1.18), lower blood mercury (β -0.06) and higher cadmium (β 0.07). These "early-life exposures" therefore partly index maternal genetic risk (passive rGE), which confounds naive exposure-outcome associations. — [Leppert et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0774)
- **Nigg J, Nikolas M, Burt SA. Measured gene-by-environment interaction in relation to ADHD. *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 2010;49:863-873. DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2010.01.025.** Review of 16 candidate-gene G×E studies. It found "relatively consistent evidence" for genotype × psychosocial-factor interaction, while G×E with pre/perinatal risks was "generally negative or unreplicated". Limitation: candidate genes that were later not replicated by GWAS. — [Nigg et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2010.01.025)
- **Contrary evidence: Østergaard SD, et al. Polygenic risk score, psychosocial environment and the risk of ADHD. *Transl Psychiatry* 2020. DOI 10.1038/s41398-020-01019-6.** iPSYCH case-cohort (Denmark). "We found no support for interaction between the polygenic liability for ADHD and the psychosocial environment upon the risk of ADHD" (parental education, work status, income and psychiatric history). The authors note that earlier positive G×E studies were small, used retrospective self-report and relied on non-replicated candidate genes. — [Østergaard et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-020-01019-6)
- Lewis et al. 2022 found no evidence that children with ADHD over-inherit polygenic liability for insomnia or chronotype. This counts against a simple shared-genetics account of ADHD-sleep comorbidity (Scite citation statement). — [Lewis et al. 2022, *Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry*, DOI 10.1007/s00787-021-01931-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-021-01931-2)

### Inferences
- Twin h² (~0.7-0.9) is much larger than SNP-h² (0.14-0.22). The gap ("missing heritability") means that current PRS explain only a small share of liability. This fits Thapar's statement that common variants have limited clinical predictive value.
- The continuum findings from twins (Larsson 2012) and GWAS (Demontis 2019) mean that the boundary of "ADHD" is a clinical threshold on a dimension, not a natural break. Changes in diagnostic thresholds, awareness or demand can therefore move large numbers of people across the line without any change in biology. This is relevant to Q5.
- Heritability estimates describe variance in a given population and era. They cannot tell us whether a population-wide environmental shift (screens, pandemic) raised mean symptom levels. A trait can be highly heritable while its mean shifts environmentally (a standard quantitative-genetics point in Visscher 2008's framework; the ADHD-specific evidence is Rydell 2018 and Sibley 2022).
- rGE (Leppert 2019) is a central methodological warning for Q2 and Q4: exposures chosen or created by parents or children (smoking, paracetamol, screen use) are partly genetically driven, so associations without genetically informed designs overstate causal effects.

### Gaps
- The exact SNP-h² from the Demontis 2023 primary text was not retrieved (Scite marked full text "restricted"). The 14% figure comes from the authors' 2023 review.
- I did not retrieve a peer-reviewed ADHD-specific paper that explicitly says "heritable does not mean unchangeable" in those words. The closest are Visscher 2008 (general) and Sibley 2022 (environment-dependent expression).
- No retrieved study directly tested whether the heritability of ADHD changed after 2020.

## 2. Environmental contributors with the strongest causal evidence

### Takeaway
The WFADHD International Consensus (2021) treats environmental factors mostly as "correlates". It states that environmental risks act very early in life, during fetal or early postnatal development, and that in rare cases extreme early deprivation, a single genetic abnormality or early traumatic brain injury can cause ADHD-like symptoms. The best-supported environmental contributors are very preterm birth / very low birth weight (about 3× odds), lead burden (small symptom correlations; about 4× odds for ADHD in one meta-analysis), and severe early institutional deprivation (a distinct, persistent "deprivation-specific" variant). Several popular "causes" shrink to the null in sibling designs: maternal smoking in pregnancy (Swedish sibling HR about 0.84-0.88) and prenatal paracetamol (sibling HR 0.98).

### Cited Findings
**WFADHD International Consensus Statement**
- **Faraone SV, Banaschewski T, Coghill D, et al. The World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement: 208 evidence-based conclusions about the disorder. *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 2021;128:789-818. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022.** Inclusion rule: studies with more than 2000 participants, or meta-analyses of 5 or more studies or 2000 or more participants, with publication bias assessed. Approved by 80 authors from 27 countries and endorsed by 366 people. Not retracted. — [WFADHD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022)
  - Quoted statements (Scite full text): "For most people with ADHD, many genetic and environmental risk factors accumulate to cause the disorder... The environmental risks for ADHD exert their effects very early in life, during the fetal or early postnatal period. In rare cases, however, ADHD-like symptoms can be caused by extreme deprivation early in life (Kennedy et al., 2016), a single genetic abnormality..., or traumatic brain injury early in life." — [WFADHD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022)
  - Lead (statement 34): Goodlad 2013 meta-analyses found small correlations of lead burden with inattention (27 studies, >9,300 youths) and with hyperactivity-impulsivity (23 studies, >7,800 youths). Nilsen & Tulve 2020 (14 studies, >17,000 children) found that higher blood lead was associated with "quadrupled odds of ADHD". — [WFADHD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022)
  - Prematurity (statement 49): threefold ADHD rate in very/extremely preterm or very/extremely low birth weight babies (Franz 2018). Low birth weight: 85 studies with >4.6 million births showed a "small-to-moderate correlation" (Momany 2018). — [WFADHD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022)
  - Maternal smoking: the association seen in large population studies "disappears after adjusting for family history of ADHD, which indicates that the association... is due to familial or genetic factors". Secondhand smoke in childhood: 9 studies, >100,000 participants, about 60% greater likelihood of ADHD (no sibling control stated). Preeclampsia is associated with about 15% greater likelihood, and over 40% with SGA. Phthalates: about 3× in the top vs. bottom maternal quintile (Engel 2018). — [WFADHD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022)
  - The Consensus places these under the heading "Environmental correlates of ADHD". It does not claim causality for most of them. — [WFADHD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022)

**Prematurity / low birth weight**
- **Franz AP, et al. ADHD and very preterm/very low birth weight: a meta-analysis. *Pediatrics* 2018;141(1):e20171645. DOI 10.1542/peds.2017-1645.** Categorical diagnosis: 12 studies (N = 1,787), OR 3.04 (95% CI 2.19-4.21). VP/VLBW OR 2.25 (1.56-3.26); EP/ELBW OR 4.05 (2.38-6.87), so the risk shows a dose-response with immaturity. Symptoms: 29 studies (N = 3,504). Inattention SMD 1.31 (0.66-1.96), hyperactivity-impulsivity SMD 0.74 (0.35-1.13), combined SMD 0.55 (0.42-0.68). Limitation: high heterogeneity in all analyses; observational studies. — [Franz et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2017-1645)

**Severe early deprivation (ERA study, "deprivation-specific" ADHD)**
- **Kennedy M, et al. Early severe institutional deprivation is associated with a persistent variant of adult ADHD... in the English and Romanian Adoptees study. *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2016;57(10):1113-1125. DOI 10.1111/jcpp.12576.** A natural experiment: UK adoptees from Romanian institutions compared with UK adoptees. Parent-reported DSM-5 criteria. At age 15, n = 108 low-deprivation (LoDep) and 86 high-deprivation (HiDep, more than 6 months institutionalised). At 22-25 years, n = 83 and 60. ADHD in LoDep was 5.6% at age 15 and 3.8% in adulthood, similar to the general population. HiDep was about 4× as likely at age 15 (19%) and over 7× as likely in young adulthood (29.3%). The deprivation variant was "equally common in males and females, was predominantly inattentive", and co-occurred with autism features, disinhibited social engagement and callous-unemotional traits. It was associated with unemployment and low education. Limitations: small n, attrition, parent report. — [Kennedy et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12576)
- **Sonuga-Barke EJS, et al. Child-to-adult neurodevelopmental and mental health trajectories after early life deprivation: the young adult follow-up of the ERA study. *Lancet* 2017;389:1539-1548. DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(17)30045-4.** Romanian adoptees with more than 6 months of deprivation (n = 98 at age 6; n = 72 in young adulthood) had persistently higher inattention/overactivity, autism and disinhibited social engagement symptoms through 22-25 years compared with UK controls (pooled p < 0.0001). Cognitive impairment remitted by adulthood. One fifth (n = 15) were problem-free at all assessments. Effects "seem insusceptible to years of nurturance and support in adoptive families". — [Sonuga-Barke et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)30045-4)

**Exposures that attenuate under familial control**
- **Skoglund C, et al. Familial confounding of the association between maternal smoking during pregnancy and ADHD in offspring. *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2014;55:61-68. DOI 10.1111/jcpp.12124.** Sweden, 813,030 births 1992-2000, register data. The population hazard ratios (moderate smoking 1.89, high 2.50) attenuated stepwise: adjusted 1.62/2.04, cousin comparison 1.45/1.69, and sibling comparison 0.88/0.84 (null). The association is attributed to unmeasured familial confounding. — [Skoglund et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12124)
- **Ahlqvist VH, et al. Acetaminophen use during pregnancy and children's risk of autism, ADHD, and intellectual disability. *JAMA* 2024;331:1205-1214. DOI 10.1001/jama.2024.3172.** Sweden, 2,480,797 children born 1995-2019. Without sibling control, ADHD HR was 1.07 (1.05-1.10). With sibling control, HR was 0.98 (0.94-1.02), with no dose-response. A later citing study (*Paediatr Perinat Epidemiol*) also found associations "nullified in the sibling comparison analyses". — [Ahlqvist et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.3172)

**Childhood maltreatment (Mendelian randomization, hypothesis-generating)**
- Ng et al. 2025 *Br J Psychiatry* (two-sample MR): genetically predicted childhood maltreatment → ADHD OR 10.09 (4.76-21.40). Leisure screen time was listed among the mediators, with 4.14-22.17% of the effect mediated by a set of factors. — [Ng et al. 2025, DOI 10.1192/bjp.2025.10433](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.2025.10433). Li et al. 2026 *Mol Psychiatry* reported MR OR 2.40 for ADHD and explicitly calls its mediation findings "hypothesis-generating". — [Li et al. 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41380-026-03912-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-026-03912-w). Caveat: "maltreatment" GWAS instruments come from adult self-report and are vulnerable to pleiotropy. The very large OR in Ng 2025 is implausible as a literal causal effect.

### Inferences
- Of the environmental contributors, only the ERA natural experiment and the prematurity dose-response give relatively strong causal leverage. ERA comes close to a natural experiment because the timing of adoption was not driven by child characteristics, and its effect is large. Even so, the ERA picture differs clinically from typical ADHD: the sex ratio is equal, it is mainly inattentive, and it co-occurs with autism and disinhibited features. It supports "environment can produce an ADHD-like presentation", not "ordinary ADHD is environmental".
- The repeated collapse of prenatal associations under sibling control (smoking, paracetamol), together with Leppert's rGE result, is a strong prior for screens (Q4). Exposures that correlate with parental ADHD genetics will show inflated observational associations.

### Gaps
- Primary lead studies (Goodlad 2013; Nilsen & Tulve 2020) and Momany 2018 were not retrieved directly. Their numbers come from the WFADHD Consensus text.
- No retrieved study gives a population-attributable fraction for environmental contributors in Europe or the Czech Republic.

## 3. Reconceptualization: ADHD as a disorder of self-regulation (attention, emotion, sleep/arousal), and formal status

### Takeaway
Several peer-reviewed proposals reframe ADHD:
- Barkley (1997) and the executive-function tradition: a core deficit in behavioral inhibition and self-regulation of affect, motivation and arousal.
- Shaw, Stringaris, Nigg et al. (2014) and Faraone et al. (2019): emotional dysregulation as a possible core feature.
- Bijlenga et al. (2019), "time to redefine ADHD?", and Hegerl & Hensch (2014): circadian disruption and unstable vigilance/arousal underlie part of the syndrome.

Evidence limits these accounts. EF deficits have only medium effect sizes and are absent in many cases ("neither necessary nor sufficient"), and no agreed, ADHD-specific definition of emotional dysregulation exists. None of the proposals has changed the formal criteria. DSM-5/DSM-5-TR lists irritability, low frustration tolerance and mood lability only as associated features. ICD-11 keeps the inattention/hyperactivity-impulsivity core and handles chronic irritability as a specifier of ODD, not as DMDD. RDoC is a research framework that includes an "Arousal and Regulatory Systems" domain but has no diagnostic status.

### Cited Findings
**Self-regulation / executive-function theory and its limits**
- **Barkley RA. Behavioral inhibition, sustained attention, and executive functions: constructing a unifying theory of ADHD. *Psychol Bull* 1997;121:65-94. DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65.** "ADHD comprises a deficit in behavioral inhibition", linked to four executive functions: working memory, "self-regulation of affect-motivation-arousal", internalization of speech and reconstitution. Evidence was "strongest for deficits in behavioral inhibition, working memory, regulation of motivation, and motor control". The author states that "far more research is required". — [Barkley 1997](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65)
- **Willcutt EG, Doyle AE, Nigg JT, Faraone SV, Pennington BF. Validity of the executive function theory of ADHD: a meta-analytic review. *Biol Psychiatry* 2005;57:1336-1346. DOI 10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006.** 83 studies; ADHD N = 3,734, controls N = 2,969. EF effect sizes were medium (0.46-0.69), strongest for response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning. "Moderate effect sizes and lack of universality of EF deficits... suggest that EF weaknesses are neither necessary nor sufficient to cause all cases of ADHD." — [Willcutt et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006)
- **Nigg JT, Willcutt EG, Doyle AE, Sonuga-Barke EJS. Causal heterogeneity in ADHD: do we need neuropsychologically impaired subtypes? *Biol Psychiatry* 2005;57:1224-1230. DOI 10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.08.025.** Group overlap means "only a subgroup may have executive deficits"; the paper proposed a provisional "executive deficit type" for DSM-V. — [Nigg et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.08.025)
- **Castellanos FX, Sonuga-Barke EJS, Milham MP, Tannock R. Characterizing cognition in ADHD: beyond executive dysfunction. *Trends Cogn Sci* 2006;10:117-123. DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2006.01.011.** Empirical findings "challenge the etiologic primacy of inhibitory and executive deficits". The authors emphasise intra-individual variability and the distinction between "hot" (affective/motivational) and "cool" EF. — [Castellanos et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2006.01.011)

**Emotional dysregulation: core or associated?**
- **Shaw P, Stringaris A, Nigg J, Leibenluft E. Emotion dysregulation in ADHD. *Am J Psychiatry* 2014;171:276-293. DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.13070966.** Review with quantitative and qualitative methods. Emotion dysregulation is "prevalent in ADHD throughout the lifespan and is a major contributor to impairment". It may arise from deficits in orienting to and recognising emotional stimuli (a striato-amygdalo-medial prefrontal network). The paper sets out three competing models: (1) correlated but distinct dimensions; (2) ED as a core diagnostic feature; (3) the combination as a distinct nosological entity. One contrasting citation exists: a later study found no deficit in perceiving emotions. — [Shaw et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.13070966)
- **Faraone SV, Rostain AL, Blader J, et al. Practitioner Review: Emotional dysregulation in ADHD. *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2019;60:133-150. DOI 10.1111/jcpp.12899.** "Some consider it to be a core feature rather than an associated trait. Others argue that emotional symptoms are too nonspecific." Emotional impulsivity and deficient emotional self-regulation "may be sufficiently specific for ADHD to function as diagnostic criteria", but "there is no consensus about how to define these constructs in a manner that would be specific to the disorder". — [Faraone et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12899)
- **Lenzi F, Cortese S, Harris J, Masi G. Pharmacotherapy of emotional dysregulation in adults with ADHD: meta-analysis. *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 2018;84:359-367. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2017.08.010.** ED is "present in up to 70% of adults with ADHD". In 21 double-blind RCTs, ADHD medications had small-to-moderate effects on ED: methylphenidate SMD 0.34 (0.23-0.45), atomoxetine 0.24 (0.15-0.34), lisdexamfetamine 0.50 (0.21-0.80). This is smaller than their effect on core symptoms. — [Lenzi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2017.08.010)

**Sleep / circadian / arousal**
- **Bijlenga D, Vollebregt MA, Kooij JJS, Arns M. The role of the circadian system in the etiology and pathophysiology of ADHD: time to redefine ADHD? *Atten Defic Hyperact Disord* 2019;11:5-19. DOI 10.1007/s12402-018-0271-z.** Delayed sleep phase disorder is "prevalent in 73-78% of children and adults with ADHD". The authors propose "a novel view on ADHD, where a part of the ADHD symptoms are the result of chronic sleep disorders, with most evidence for the delayed circadian rhythm as the underlying mechanism". They frame this as a "substantial subgroup". This is a review and position paper, not a test. — [Bijlenga et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-018-0271-z)
- **Hegerl U, Hensch T. The vigilance regulation model of affective disorders and ADHD. *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 2014;44:45-57. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2012.10.008.** Hyperactivity and sensation-seeking are interpreted as an "autoregulatory attempt to stabilise vigilance by creating a stimulating environment". It proposes unstable EEG vigilance as a pathophysiology shared by ADHD and mania. This is a theoretical model. — [Hegerl & Hensch 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2012.10.008)
- Disconfirming genetics: children with ADHD did not over-inherit polygenic liability for insomnia or chronotype (Lewis et al. 2022). — [Lewis et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-021-01931-2)

**Formal status**
- **DSM-5 / DSM-5-TR (APA):** emotional symptoms are "associated features supporting diagnosis" ("low frustration tolerance, irritability, or mood lability"), not criteria. Emotional symptoms became associated features, rather than criteria, from DSM-III onward. Source quality: secondary copies and summaries via WebSearch; the official DSM-5-TR text was not retrieved. — [BMC table reproducing DSM-5 criteria](https://bpded.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40479-018-0086-8/tables/1); [Shaw et al. 2014, Focus reprint](https://focus.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.focus.140102)
- **ICD-11 (WHO), 6A05 ADHD:** presentations are predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, combined, other specified and unspecified. In a mirror of the WHO text, the "Additional clinical features" do not list emotional dysregulation (WebSearch summary; the official ICD-11 browser and CDDR were not retrieved). — [ICD-11 6A05 mirror](https://www.findacode.com/icd-11/code-821852937.html)
- **ICD-11 vs DMDD:** WHO placed chronic irritability inside ODD ("ODD with chronic irritability-anger") rather than creating DMDD. DSM-5 created DMDD as a separate childhood mood/depressive diagnosis. In a 48-country vignette field study, the ICD-11 approach identified severe irritability more accurately; DSM-5 users often failed to assign DMDD when it applied and more often pathologised normative irritability. Source: Evans SC, et al. *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2021;62(3):303-312, DOI 10.1111/jcpp.13244 (via WebSearch summary; abstract not retrieved via PubMed). — [Evans et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.13244)
- **DMDD epidemiology and overlap:** Copeland WE, Angold A, Costello EJ, Egger H. *Am J Psychiatry* 2013;170:173-179, DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12010132. Three US community studies (3,258 participants aged 2-17; 7,881 observations). 3-month DMDD prevalence was 0.8-3.3%, highest in preschoolers. DMDD co-occurred with another disorder 62-92% of the time; co-occurrence with ODD was highest (OR 52.9-103.0), and with depression OR 9.9-23.5. — [Copeland et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12010132)
- **RDoC (NIMH):** RDoC is a research framework. Its "Arousal and Regulatory Systems" domain originally proposed the constructs Arousal, Biological Rhythms and Default Readiness; a 2012 workshop added Sleep and Wakefulness. The current matrix lists six domains. RDoC does not define ADHD. — [NIMH RDoC Arousal/Regulatory workshop proceedings](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/arousal-and-regulatory-systems-workshop-proceedings)

### Inferences
- The self-regulation framing has respectable peer-reviewed proponents: Barkley, Shaw/Stringaris/Nigg/Leibenluft, Faraone et al., Bijlenga/Kooij/Arns, Hegerl. Its status is that of a *theoretical/heuristic reframing and a research agenda*. It has not been codified in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria, and RDoC only provides adjacent research constructs.
- The heterogeneity evidence (Willcutt 2005, Nigg 2005, Castellanos 2006) argues against any single-mechanism redefinition, whether "attention", "EF" or "circadian". It favours multiple pathways, some of which run through self-regulation, emotion or sleep.
- For the screens question, the arousal/vigilance model (Hegerl) predicts that high-stimulation media seeking could be a *consequence* or *self-regulation strategy* of ADHD. That prediction matches the reverse-direction findings in Q4, but it was not directly tested in the retrieved studies.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper in this task that explicitly uses the word "misnomer" for "attention deficit". Scite check_claim returned no relevant statement. The claim is commonly attributed to Barkley's books (not peer-reviewed) and was not verified.
- Exact DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 wording was not read from primary sources (WebFetch blocked or not attempted). It should be verified before quoting.
- Emotional dysregulation was not added as a criterion in DSM-5-TR (2022); this is per the secondary sources, and the TR text was not checked directly.

## 4. Screen media: cause of ADHD vs. cause of ADHD-like symptoms vs. consequence/self-regulation strategy of ADHD

### Takeaway
1. **Screens as a cause of the disorder (diagnosis):** not supported. The longitudinal Dutch KOALA cohort found no association between early screen time and later ADHD diagnosis. Genetically informed designs (PRS, Gsens) attribute most or all of the screen-attention association to shared genetic liability. Two Mendelian randomization studies point in opposite directions: one found screen time increased ADHD risk, the other found it decreased risk.
2. **Screens increasing symptom levels:** a small, fairly consistent prospective association exists (meta-analytic r about 0.11-0.12; per-activity OR 1.10 in Ra 2018). The strongest within-person evidence concerns social media and inattention/impulsivity (Wallace 2023; Nivins 2026). Effects are small, and part of them is genetic confounding.
3. **Screen overuse as a consequence or marker of ADHD:** supported. ADHD PRS predicts more screen time (ABCD, Japan, MoBa), and longitudinal reviews show selection effects. Problematic use is more strongly linked to ADHD than duration is.

### Cited Findings
**Meta-analyses and systematic reviews (association level)**
- **Nikkelen SWC, Valkenburg PM, Huizinga M, Bushman BJ. Media use and ADHD-related behaviors in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. *Dev Psychol* 2014;50:2228-2241. DOI 10.1037/a0037318.** 45 empirical studies; "small significant relationship" r+ = 0.12. — [Nikkelen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037318)
- **Beyens I, Valkenburg PM, Piotrowski JT. Screen media use and ADHD-related behaviors: four decades of research. *PNAS* 2018;115:9875-9881. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1611611114.** Systematic review: "a statistically small relationship". It is moderated by gender and trait aggression, and the authors call for research "that investigates causality". — [Beyens et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611611114)
- **Eirich R, et al. Association of screen time with internalizing and externalizing behavior problems in children ≤12 years. *JAMA Psychiatry* 2022;79(5):393-405. DOI 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0155.** 87 studies, 98 samples, 159,425 children (mean age 6.07), pre-pandemic. Externalizing problems, which include ADHD symptoms, r = 0.11 (0.10-0.12); internalizing r = 0.07 (0.05-0.08); I² = 87.80; methodological moderators. — [Eirich et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0155)
- **Thorell LB, et al. Longitudinal associations between digital media use and ADHD symptoms in children and adolescents: a systematic literature review. *Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 2024;33(8):2503-2526 (online 2022). DOI 10.1007/s00787-022-02130-3.** 28 longitudinal studies (2011-2021). There was "support for reciprocal associations". Associations were "more consistent for problematic use of digital media than for screen time". Both "selection effects" (children with ADHD symptoms are more vulnerable to high or problematic use) and "media effects" (direct, or via sleep and social relations) are present. — [Thorell et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-022-02130-3)
- Fabio et al. 2026 *Res Dev Disabil*, systematic review of social media use: 25 studies, mostly cross-sectional. Problematic social media use is associated with inattention and impulsivity, and "the predominance of cross-sectional designs limits conclusions about directionality"; no formal risk-of-bias assessment. — [Fabio et al. 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.ridd.2026.105237](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2026.105237)

**Prospective cohorts (symptom level)**
- **Ra CK, Cho J, Stone MD, et al. Association of digital media use with subsequent symptoms of ADHD among adolescents. *JAMA* 2018;320(3):255-263. DOI 10.1001/jama.2018.8931.** USA (10 Los Angeles high schools, convenience sample). 2,587 15-16-year-olds without significant ADHD symptoms at baseline, followed for 24 months. Exposure: number of 14 digital activities used "many times a day". Outcome: self-rated DSM symptoms, with ≥6 in either domain counted as "symptom-positive". OR per additional high-frequency activity was 1.11 (1.06-1.16), adjusted 1.10 (1.05-1.15). 4.6% of those with no high-frequency activities became symptom-positive, vs 9.5% with 7 and 10.5% with 14 activities (difference 5.9%, 2.6-9.2). The authors call the association "statistically significant but modest" and state that "further research is needed to determine whether this association is causal". Limitations: self-report of both exposure and outcome, no clinical diagnosis, residual confounding, no genetic control. — [Ra et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.8931)
- **Tamana SK, et al. Screen-time is associated with inattention problems in preschoolers: CHILD birth cohort. *PLoS One* 2019. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0213995.** Canada, 2,322 children, parent-reported CBCL at age 5. More than 2 h/day vs less than 30 min: externalizing T-score +2.2 (0.9-3.5); clinically significant inattention 5.9× (1.6-21.5); "7.7-fold increased risk of meeting criteria for ADHD (95% CI 1.6-38.1)", based on only n = 24 above the cut-off. Limitations: very wide CIs, mostly cross-sectional, same informant. One contrasting citation (a small Sri Lankan sample) found no association. — [Tamana et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0213995)
- **Levelink B, et al. The longitudinal relationship between screen time, sleep and a diagnosis of ADHD in childhood. *J Atten Disord* 2021 (online 2020). DOI 10.1177/1087054720953897.** Netherlands, KOALA birth cohort, 2,768 mother-child pairs. Screen time and sleep at ages 2, 4 and 6 vs ADHD at 8-10. **"Longitudinally, neither screen time nor sleep were associated with ADHD."** Cross-sectionally, CBCL externalizing rose 0.03 per hour of TV at age 2. A null result for the *disorder* outcome. — [Levelink et al. 2020/2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054720953897)
- **Wallace J, et al. Screen time, impulsivity, neuropsychological functions and their relationship to growth in adolescent ADHD symptoms. *Sci Rep* 2023. DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-44105-7.** Canada, about 4,000 high-school students over 5 years; multilevel mediation with between- and within-person decomposition. Year-to-year increases in screen time were associated with same-year exacerbation of ADHD symptoms (within-person), "over and above potential common vulnerability". Only social media showed a *lagged* within-person effect, mediated by impulsivity and Go/No-Go response inhibition. Limitation: self-report; no genetic control. — [Wallace et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-44105-7)
- **Nagata JM, et al. Screen time and mental health: prospective analysis of the ABCD Study. *BMC Public Health* 2024. DOI 10.1186/s12889-024-20102-x.** USA, 9,538 children aged 9-10, 2-year follow-up. Screen time → parent-reported ADHD symptoms B = 0.06 (0.01-0.10). The association was stronger for depressive symptoms (B = 0.10), and the authors note that "effect sizes are small". — [Nagata et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-024-20102-x)
- **Shou et al. 2025 *Transl Psychiatry*, DOI 10.1038/s41398-025-03672-1.** ABCD, 10,116 at baseline and 7,880 at 2 years. Baseline screen time → increase in ADHD symptoms β = 0.032 (p = 0.001), plus small cortical thickness differences. — [Shou et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-025-03672-1)
- **Nivins S, et al. Digital media, genetics, and risk for ADHD symptoms in children: a longitudinal study. *Pediatr Open Sci* 2026. DOI 10.1542/pedsos.2025-000922.** ABCD, 8,324 children (mean age 9.9), 4 years, with adjustment for ADHD PGS and SES. Social media use → increase in inattention β = 0.03/yr, cumulative 4-year β = 0.15. **No association for video games or TV/videos.** The effect was not moderated by PGS, ADHD diagnosis or medication, and **inattention did not predict later social media use**. The authors call the effect "small". — [Nivins et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1542/pedsos.2025-000922)

**Genetic confounding and reverse causation (ADHD → screens)**
- **Zhang Y, et al. Shared genetic risk in the association of screen time with psychiatric problems in children. *JAMA Netw Open* 2023;6(11):e2341502. DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.41502.** ABCD, 4,262 children of European ancestry aged 9-11. Screen time → attention problems β = 0.10 SD (0.07-0.13). The ADHD PRS was also associated with screen time (cross-trait). Gsens modelling: genetic confounding "accounted for most of the association between child screen time and attention problems" using SNP-h², and "fully explained" it using twin-h². — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.41502)
- **Yang A, et al. Longer screen time utilization is associated with the polygenic risk for ADHD... *EBioMedicine* 2022. DOI 10.1016/j.ebiom.2022.104039.** ABCD, more than 11,000 children aged 9-11. Higher ADHD PRS → longer screen time. The longitudinal effect between the ADHD scale and screen time was bidirectional. — [Yang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2022.104039)
- **Takahashi N, et al. The association between screen time and genetic risks for NDDs in children. *Psychiatry Res* 2023. DOI 10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115395.** Longitudinal birth cohort (Japan), ages 24-40 months. ADHD genetic risk was associated with an *increase* in screen time over time; the authors conclude that "prolonged screen time may not be a cause... but an early sign of NDDs". — [Takahashi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115395)
- **Frei E, et al. The phenotypic and genetic relationship between adolescent mental health and time spent on social media, gaming, and TV. *Psychiatry Res* 2025. DOI 10.1016/j.psychres.2025.116761.** Norway (MoBa), 23,790 adolescents aged 14-16, with register diagnoses. 3-4 h/day or more on any screen activity was associated with hyperkinetic disorder, depression and anxiety. *Minimal* social media use was also associated with diagnoses, especially autism. ADHD PGS was associated with screen use, and "a substantial proportion of the phenotypic associations appeared to be attributable to genetic confounding". — [Frei et al. 2025a](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2025.116761)
- Frei et al. 2025 *Sci Rep* (MoBa GWAS, n = 18,490): screen behaviors are heritable (SNP-h² 0.048-0.12) and genetically correlated with psychiatric disorders including ADHD (rg 0.21-0.42). — [Frei et al. 2025b, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-17450-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17450-y)

**Mendelian randomization (conflicting)**
- Meng et al. 2024 *Front Psychiatry*, two-sample bidirectional MR (PGC + UK Biobank): mobile phone use → childhood ADHD OR 1.848 (1.336-2.556); TV OR 2.104 (1.396-3.170); no reverse effect found. A published Commentary (Takahashi et al. 2024, DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1512149) responded; its content was not retrieved. — [Meng et al. 2024, DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1441191](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1441191)
- **Contradicting:** Cai et al. 2025 *Brain Behav*, MR with 703,901 participants: each SD increase in genetically predicted leisure screen time → **lower** ADHD risk, OR 0.68 (0.52-0.89); weighted median OR 0.68 (0.47-0.98). — [Cai et al. 2025, DOI 10.1002/brb3.70884](https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.70884)

**Pandemic context**
- Rogers MA, MacLean J. ADHD symptoms increased during the Covid-19 pandemic: a meta-analysis. *J Atten Disord* 2023;27(8):800-811. DOI 10.1177/10870547231158750. 18 studies (12 longitudinal, 6 retrospective), 6,491 participants, 10 countries. Children and caregivers reported increased ADHD symptoms; the abstract gives no pooled effect size, and the mechanisms (screens, routine loss, sleep, school closure) were not separated. — [Rogers & MacLean 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547231158750)

### Inferences
- **"Screens cause ADHD (the disorder)":** no retrieved study supports this with a causal design. The only longitudinal study with a *diagnosis* outcome (KOALA) was null. Genetic-confounding analyses explain most or all of the screen-attention association. MR results contradict each other, which is typical of weak or pleiotropic instruments built from adult UK Biobank "leisure screen time".
- **"Screens raise ADHD-like symptom levels":** plausible at small magnitude, especially for social media and adolescent inattention/impulsivity. The within-person (Wallace) and PGS-adjusted (Nivins) designs are the strongest evidence. Such effects could shift the population mean slightly and push borderline individuals over a threshold. This is consistent with the dimensional model in Q1, but no study quantified how many diagnoses this would produce.
- **"Screen overuse as a symptom or self-regulation strategy":** the PRS → screen-time findings (Zhang, Yang, Takahashi, Frei), the selection effects (Thorell) and the stronger link with *problematic* than with *duration* of use fit this. They also fit the vigilance/arousal model (Hegerl) in which stimulation-seeking regulates arousal, though that mechanism was not directly tested.
- The three claims are not mutually exclusive. Current evidence ranks them (3) ≥ (2) >> (1).

### Gaps
- I found no co-twin-control or sibling-comparison study of screen time → ADHD *diagnosis* (Scite search for twin, co-twin and within-family designs returned none). This is a key missing design.
- I found no RCT of screen-time reduction with ADHD symptoms or diagnosis as the outcome in this search.
- Pandemic-era screen exposure and later ADHD diagnosis were not linked in any retrieved study.
- The Takahashi 2024 commentary on Meng's MR was not read.

## 5. Diagnostic and prescription trends 2015-2026 (pre-, during and post-pandemic); overdiagnosis; relative age; social media

### Takeaway
True community prevalence of ADHD meeting standardized criteria has been flat for decades (Polanczyk 2014), and the extreme phenotype in Swedish twins did not change between 2004 and 2014 while clinical diagnoses rose more than fivefold (Rydell 2018). Diagnoses and medication use were already rising before 2020, mainly in adults; global child consumption grew about 9.7% per year in 2015-2019. After an early-2020 dip, they accelerated sharply from 2020 to 2024, most of all among adolescent girls and young women (Finland, Norway, Ontario, US, England, France). In US children, stimulant fills fell slightly between 2019 and 2023, partly because of shortages. Overdiagnosis and overtreatment of milder cases is documented (Kazda 2021), and so is a robust relative-age effect. Social media content on ADHD is often misleading, but its causal role in diagnosis rates is not established. Czech data show rising diagnoses and methylphenidate use, but only news and insurer-level figures were accessible.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence vs diagnosis (long-run)**
- **Polanczyk GV, Willcutt EG, Salum GA, Kieling C, Rohde LA. ADHD prevalence estimates across three decades. *Int J Epidemiol* 2014;43:434-442. DOI 10.1093/ije/dyt261.** Meta-regression of 135 studies. "Geographical location and year of study were not associated with variability in ADHD prevalence estimates... no evidence to suggest an increase in the number of children in the community who meet criteria for ADHD when standardized diagnostic procedures are followed." — [Polanczyk et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyt261)
- **Rydell M, Lundström S, Gillberg C, Lichtenstein P, Larsson H. Has the ADHD phenotype become more common in children between 2004 and 2014? *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2018;59:863-871. DOI 10.1111/jcpp.12882.** Sweden (CATSS), 19,271 9-year-old twins. Parent-rated diagnostic-level ADHD did not change significantly (OR 1.37, 0.77-2.45). Subthreshold ADHD and mean scores rose slightly (p < .001). **Clinically diagnosed ADHD rose more than fivefold (OR 5.27, 1.85-14.96).** The authors attribute this to changes in diagnostic practice, awareness, access or "current overdiagnosis". — [Rydell et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12882)

### Inferences
- The pattern of flat true prevalence (Polanczyk), a flat extreme phenotype (Rydell), rising diagnoses and the relative-age effect points to changes in recognition and thresholds as the main driver of rising diagnoses before 2020, not a rise in the underlying disorder.
- The 2020-2024 acceleration is concentrated in adolescent girls and young adult women (Finland, Norway, Ontario, US MMWR), with little or no increment in young boys (Finland). This demographic profile is more consistent with catch-up recognition of previously under-diagnosed groups, telehealth access, and awareness/help-seeking (possibly social-media-influenced) than with a screen-induced childhood neurodevelopmental disorder. The latter would be expected to show up first in young children of both sexes. This is an inference; no study tested these mechanisms against each other.
- The pandemic increases in medication do not by themselves distinguish appropriate catch-up treatment from overdiagnosis. Kazda 2021 shows that the evidence on benefits and harms for milder cases is very thin (5 studies).

## 6. What behavioral/environmental interventions achieve for diagnosed ADHD

### Takeaway
Parent training and behavioral interventions reliably improve **parenting, parent-child relations and conduct problems**, including under probably-blinded assessment. Their effect on **core ADHD symptoms** largely disappears when outcomes are rated blind. They reduce impairment and associated problems but do not normalize ADHD. In the MTA trial, medication management beat intensive behavioral treatment for core symptoms at 14 months. By 6-8 years and in adulthood, the original randomized groups no longer differed, MTA participants remained worse off than peers on 91% of variables, and only 9.1% showed sustained recovery. Guidelines reflect this. Behavioral or parent training is first-line for preschoolers (AAP; NICE group parent training for under-5s). For school-age children NICE found no clear evidence that formal parent training improves ADHD symptoms, and it recommends environmental modifications before medication. Severe early deprivation shows the limits of later environmental enrichment: its effects persisted despite years in adoptive families.

### Cited Findings
**The MTA study**
- **MTA Cooperative Group. A 14-month randomized clinical trial of treatment strategies for ADHD. *Arch Gen Psychiatry* 1999;56:1073-1086. DOI 10.1001/archpsyc.56.12.1073.** USA/Canada, 579 children aged 7-9.9 with combined-type ADHD. Arms: medication management, intensive behavioral treatment, combined, and community care. Medication management was superior to behavioral treatment and community care for core ADHD symptoms. Combined treatment was not significantly better than medication for core symptoms but "may have provided modest advantages for non-ADHD symptom and positive functioning outcomes" (oppositional, internalizing, social skills, parent-child relations, reading). — [MTA 1999](https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.56.12.1073)
- **Molina BSG, et al. The MTA at 8 years. *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 2009;48:484-500. DOI 10.1097/CHI.0b013e31819c23d0.** N = 436. At 6 and 8 years "the originally randomized treatment groups did not differ significantly". Medication use fell 62% after the trial. Symptom trajectory in the first 3 years predicted 55% of outcomes. Participants "fared worse than the local normative comparison group on 91% of the variables tested". — [Molina et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1097/CHI.0b013e31819c23d0)
- **Swanson JM, et al. Young adult outcomes in the MTA follow-up: symptom persistence, source discrepancy, and height suppression. *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2017;58:663-678. DOI 10.1111/jcpp.12684.** 515 ADHD participants and 289 local comparison classmates, followed to age 25. Symptom persistence vs the comparison group: d = 1.11. Consistent or inconsistent long-term stimulant use was associated with being 2.55 cm shorter (d = .42) but **not** with reduced symptom severity (naturalistic, non-randomized subgroups). — [Swanson et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12684)
- **Sibley MH, et al. Variable patterns of remission from ADHD in the MTA. *Am J Psychiatry* 2022;179:142-151. DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21010032.** N = 558, eight assessments up to age about 25. About 30% reached full remission at some point, and 60% of those then had a recurrence. 9.1% showed sustained recovery and 10.8% stable persistence; 63.8% fluctuated. "90% of children with ADHD in MTA continued to experience residual symptoms into young adulthood." — [Sibley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21010032)

**Meta-analyses of behavioral and parent training (blinded vs unblinded)**
- **Sonuga-Barke EJS, et al. (European ADHD Guidelines Group). Nonpharmacological interventions for ADHD: meta-analyses of RCTs of dietary and psychological treatments. *Am J Psychiatry* 2013;170:275-289. DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991.** 54 RCTs. With the "most proximal" (usually unblinded) raters, psychological treatments had SMD 0.40-0.64. With probably-blinded assessments, the effects of behavioral interventions, neurofeedback and cognitive training "were substantially attenuated to nonsignificant levels". Free fatty acids (SMD 0.16) and artificial food colour exclusion (0.42) remained significant. One contrasting citation (a 2019 *Psychol Bull* meta-analysis) found large effects on working memory and inhibition for some interventions. — [Sonuga-Barke et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991)
- **Daley D, et al. Behavioral interventions in ADHD: a meta-analysis of RCTs across multiple outcome domains. *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 2014;53:835-847. DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2014.05.013.** 32 RCTs. Unblinded outcomes: positive parenting SMD 0.68, negative parenting 0.57, child ADHD 0.35, conduct 0.26, social skills 0.47, academic 0.28. **Probably blinded outcomes: positive parenting 0.63, negative parenting 0.43, conduct problems 0.31; there was no blinded evidence of an ADHD symptom decrease.** — [Daley et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2014.05.013)
- **Dekkers TJ, et al. Meta-analysis: which components of parent training work for children with ADHD? *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 2022;61:478-494. DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2021.06.015.** 29 RCTs, N = 2,345. "Robust small- to medium-sized positive effects on all parental outcomes... both for unblinded and probably blinded measures." Antecedent manipulation and reinforcement were the key techniques; more psychoeducation was associated with *worse* parental outcomes. — [Dekkers et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2021.06.015)
- Adults, for comparison: **Ostinelli EG, et al. *Lancet Psychiatry* 2025;12:32-43. DOI 10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00360-2.** Component network meta-analysis, 113 RCTs, 14,887 adults. Only stimulants and atomoxetine reduced core symptoms on both self- and clinician-rated scales. CBT (SMD -0.76), mindfulness (-0.79), psychoeducation (-0.77) and cognitive remediation beat placebo only on clinician-rated scales. Medications did not improve quality of life. — [Ostinelli et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00360-2)
- Medication evidence is also uncertain: in the Cochrane review by Storebø et al. 2025 (212 trials), methylphenidate improved teacher-rated symptoms with SMD -0.74 (-0.88 to -0.61), at very low certainty. 191 of 212 trials were at high risk of bias, and all were at high risk if deblinding is considered. — [Storebø et al. 2025, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD009885.pub4](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009885.pub4)

**Guidelines**
- **NICE NG87 (2018, updated 2019), UK** (WebSearch summary of the NICE "Rationale and impact" page; full recommendations not fetched):
  - Under-5s: "clinically important benefit on some measures of symptoms of an ADHD-focused group parent-training programme". Group parent training is the first-line approach, and clinicians should check "that environmental modifications have been done before starting medication".

Methylphenidate may be considered if behavior therapy gives no significant improvement and moderate-to-severe symptoms persist; the AAFP summary specifies "at least nine months of moderate to severe dysfunction".
  - Recommendation numbering and grades were not verified; the source is a WebSearch summary of AAP, AAFP and CDC pages.
  — [AAP guideline](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2019-2528); [AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0701/p58.html); [AAP EQIPP key action statements](https://eqipp.aap.org/courses/adhd/mn/clinical-guide/popups/key-action-statements)

**Environmental enrichment after severe deprivation**
- ERA: effects of extended early deprivation (inattention/overactivity, autism features, disinhibited social engagement) "seem insusceptible to years of nurturance and support in adoptive families". However, one fifth of the high-deprivation adoptees were problem-free at every assessment. — [Sonuga-Barke et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)30045-4)

### Inferences
- Behavioral and environmental interventions mainly **reduce impairment and secondary problems**: oppositional and conduct behaviour, parenting stress and family functioning. They do not **normalize core ADHD symptoms**. Because the disorder is defined by those symptoms, intervention evidence does not suggest that environment-only changes, screen reduction included, would "cure" diagnosed ADHD. Small within-person symptom effects (Q4) could still matter for impairment.
- The MTA long-term data show convergence across the randomized arms and persisting group differences from peers. Together with Sibley's fluctuating course, this fits a heritable, dimensional, context-sensitive trait more than a fixed lesion or a purely environmentally induced condition.
- Medication's short-term superiority for core symptoms coexists with very-low-certainty evidence (Cochrane) and with no demonstrated long-term symptom advantage in naturalistic MTA follow-up. The "behavior vs medication" contrast is therefore about *which outcomes* change, not about one option normalizing ADHD.

### Gaps

- The full-text NICE NG87 and AAP 2019 recommendations were not fetched directly. Wording, numbering and grades should be verified before quoting.
- Current European guideline texts (e.g. the German S3 guideline, an updated European ADHD Guidelines Group statement) were not retrieved. The European evidence here rests on the EAGG meta-analyses (Sonuga-Barke 2013; Daley 2014).
- Long-term (more than 2 years) blinded outcomes of parent training on ADHD symptoms were not retrieved.
